What is a wet timing belt?

A wet belt runs inside the engine, submerged in oil, rather than behind a cover on the outside. Manufacturers moved to them for efficiency and quietness. The problem is that oil and modern fuels break the rubber down, and as it degrades it sheds material into the sump that blocks the oil pickup screen. Once oil flow is choked, bearing damage follows, and by the time a warning light appears the damage is usually done.

Why do intervals keep getting shortened?

Because the original ones were optimistic. Ford cut the 2.0 EcoBlue belt from 10 years or 150,000 miles to 6 years or 100,000 miles, and Stellantis shortened the 1.2 PureTech to 6 years or 100,000 km. Fuel getting into the oil during DPF regeneration accelerates the breakdown, which was not fully accounted for at launch.

How accurate is this checker?

If an engine came in more than one timing setup, or the published sources disagree, we tell you that instead of guessing. What should I do if I have a wet belt?

Do not panic, but do not ignore it either. Get the belt condition and the oil pickup inspected, and plan the replacement rather than waiting for a symptom. If the engine has been serviced late, or has done a lot of short journeys, treat it as more urgent. We are happy to talk it through on the phone with no obligation.
